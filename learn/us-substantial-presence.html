US substantial presence test: the weighted 183-day formula

You meet the US substantial presence test for a calendar year if you spent at least 31 days physically in the US in that year and also reach 183 days over the 3-year period that includes it. The count takes every day in the current year, one third of the days in the year before, and one sixth of the days in the year before that. Meeting the test generally makes you a US tax resident, unless an exception such as the closer connection exception applies.

Quick answers

Which days count as present?

A day counts when you are physically in the US at any time during it. Some days are excluded, such as commuting from Canada or Mexico, a transit of under 24 hours between two places outside the US, crew of a foreign vessel, days when a medical condition that began in the US stopped you leaving, and days as an exempt individual.

Who is an exempt individual?

Examples include people connected to a foreign government on A or G visas, teachers and trainees holding J or Q visas, and students holding F, J, M or Q visas, provided they substantially comply with their visa requirements. If you exclude days this way you generally must file Form 8843.

Can I meet the test and still be a nonresident?

Possibly. The closer connection exception may apply if you spend fewer than 183 days in the US in the current year and your ties to a foreign country are closer. See the IRS pages and Form 8840.

Does it only count days in the 50 states?

The US for this test includes the 50 states, the District of Columbia and US territorial waters. It does not include US territories or airspace.

Worked examples

IRS example. You were present 120 days in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025. For 2025: 120 + (120 ÷ 3 = 40) + (120 ÷ 6 = 20) = 180. That is less than 183, so the test is not met for 2025.

A second case. 150 days in 2025, 90 in 2024 and 90 in 2023: 150 + 30 + 15 = 195. You were present at least 31 days in 2025 and the total is at least 183, so you meet the test, subject to exceptions.
