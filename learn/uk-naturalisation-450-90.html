UK naturalisation: the 450 and 90 day absence limits

For naturalisation as a British citizen under the 5-year route (section 6(1), British Nationality Act 1981), Home Office guidance caps absences at 450 days across the 5-year period and 90 days across the 12 months before the application. Only whole days are counted, and the days of departure and arrival are not counted as absences. Discretion can be exercised for excess absences, but you should not rely on it.

Quick answers

How are absence days counted?

Only whole days count, and the days of departure and arrival are excluded. The guidance's example: going away on 22 September and coming back on 23 September counts as no absence at all.

What else is required for the 5-year route?

You must have been in the UK when the 5-year period began, and must not be subject to an immigration time restriction on the application date, among other requirements. Holders of indefinite leave are treated as meeting the time-restriction rule.

What if I am over the limit?

The guidance says discretion must be exercised if you exceed the permitted absence by 30 days or less, unless there are other grounds for refusal. Larger excesses are considered only in limited circumstances, and refusal is usual beyond certain levels.

Is there a different limit for spouses of British citizens?

The 3-year route for people whose spouse or civil partner is a British citizen (section 6(2)) has a lower absence limit than the 5-year route. Check the guidance for the current figures.

Is this the same as the ILR rule?

No. Indefinite leave uses the 180 days in 12 months continuous residence rule, linked below.

Worked example

You leave the UK on 1 June and return on 11 June. Excluding the departure day and the arrival day, the absence is 2 June to 10 June, which is 9 days.

Suppose your total absences over the 5 years are 440 days, under 450, but 95 of them fall in the 12 months before you apply. The 90-day last-year limit is exceeded by 5 days. The guidance says discretion must be exercised where the excess is 30 days or less, unless there are other grounds for refusal, but the outcome depends on your whole application.
