Canadian citizenship: the 1,095 days physical presence rule

To apply for Canadian citizenship as an adult you must have spent at least 1,095 days (3 years) physically in Canada during the 5-year eligibility period before the day you sign your application. According to IRCC, that period must include at least 730 days as a permanent resident.

Quick answers

Which 5 years are counted?

The 5 years before the day you sign your application.

Do days as a temporary resident count?

Partly. IRCC says each day as a temporary resident or protected person during the eligibility period counts as 0.5 days, with a maximum of 365 days credited.

Does a day as a permanent resident count fully?

Yes. IRCC's physical presence guidance counts each day you were in Canada as a permanent resident as a full day.

Is there a calculator?

Yes. IRCC provides a physical presence calculator in your online account, and a paper form for paper applications. Use it to confirm your count.

Should I apply with exactly 1,095 days?

IRCC recommends having more than 1,095 days in case there is a problem with the calculation.

Worked example

Over the 5 years before you sign, you spent 730 days in Canada as a temporary resident and then 730 days in Canada as a permanent resident.

  • Permanent resident days: 730 (meets the 730 minimum).
  • Temporary resident days: 730 × 0.5 = 365, which is the maximum credit.
  • Total: 730 + 365 = 1,095 days, which just meets the requirement.

Someone with 1,000 days in Canada as a permanent resident and 200 as a temporary resident has 1,000 + 100 = 1,100 days. Always confirm with IRCC's own calculator.
