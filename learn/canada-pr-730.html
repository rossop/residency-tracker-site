Canadian permanent residents: the 730 days in 5 years rule

To keep permanent resident (PR) status you must have been in Canada for at least 730 days during the last five years. The 730 days do not need to be continuous. Some time spent outside Canada can also count, for example when you are with a spouse, common-law partner or parent who is a Canadian citizen, or when you work full-time for a Canadian business. If you fall short you remain a PR until an official decision is made on your status.

Quick answers

Do the 730 days need to be in a row?

No. IRCC says the 730 days do not need to be continuous.

Which days outside Canada count?

Under section 28 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, days also count when you are outside Canada accompanying a Canadian citizen who is your spouse, common-law partner or parent, or when you work full-time for a Canadian business or the public service. There are further cases, so read the section.

What counts as a day in Canada?

IRCC's enforcement manual ENF 23 says a day includes a full day or any part of a day physically in Canada.

What if I am short?

You do not lose status automatically. IRCC says you stay a PR until an official decision is made on your status. Humanitarian and compassionate factors can be considered. You may face delays at the border, and can apply for a permanent resident travel document.

Is this the citizenship test?

No. Citizenship has a different test: see the 1,095 days page linked below.

Worked example

In your most recent five years as a PR you spent 500 days in Canada and 250 days outside Canada accompanying your Canadian citizen spouse.

500 + 250 = 750 days, which is at least 730, so the residency obligation is met for that period. With 500 days in Canada and 200 days outside accompanying your spouse, the total is 700, which falls short and would need an officer's assessment of other factors.
