Irish naturalisation: the 70 day absence allowance

To naturalise as an Irish citizen you need a continuous period of residence in Ireland straight before you apply, and absences of up to 70 days in that year are still reckoned as residence. Under section 15C of the 1956 Act, a further 30 days can be reckoned if the Minister is satisfied the absence was caused by exceptional circumstances relating to you. In the Act, a "day" means a whole day only: part of a day does not count.

Quick answers

What is the full residence requirement?

Immigration Service Delivery guidance describes reckonable residence of 5 years in the last 9 years, with 1 year of continuous residence immediately before the application, for most applicants. Spouses of Irish citizens and refugees have different periods. Read the official guidance for your category.

Do departure and return days count as absence?

The Act treats a day as a whole day only, and Immigration Service Delivery guidance says that neither your departure day nor your return day counts as an absence.

Is the extra 30 days automatic?

No. It is for the Minister to decide, and only where the absence was caused by exceptional circumstances relating to you. Do not assume it will be granted.

What else can stop time counting?

Time without valid immigration permission, and some permissions such as certain student permissions, may not count as reckonable residence. Check the official guidance.

Worked example

In the year before you apply you are abroad for 45 days in the spring and 20 days in the autumn: 65 days in total. That is under 70, so the absences are reckoned as residence.

If your absences total 85 days, you are 15 over the 70-day allowance. The extra days count only if the Minister accepts that exceptional circumstances relating to you caused them, up to a further 30 days. If you are over 100 days, the extra allowance cannot cover it.
