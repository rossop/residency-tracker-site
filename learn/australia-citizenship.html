Australian citizenship: the residence requirement

To be eligible for Australian citizenship by conferral under the general residence requirement, the Department of Home Affairs says you must have been living in Australia on a valid visa for the 4 years immediately before the day you apply, held a permanent visa (or a Special Category subclass 444 visa) for the last 12 months immediately before you apply, and not been absent from Australia for more than 12 months in the past 4 years, including no more than 90 days in the 12 months immediately before applying.

Quick answers

Which period is counted?

The 4 years immediately before the day you apply, with the last 12 months looked at separately for the 90-day absence limit and the permanent visa requirement.

Does the department provide a calculator?

Yes, a Residence Calculator that needs exact dates of your time in and out of Australia. The department says to use its results as a guide only, because the assessment is based on the data it holds when you lodge.

Are there exemptions?

The department says exemptions, discretions or special circumstances may apply in some cases, and special rules exist for certain New Zealand citizens. Check the department's pages for your situation.

Is the residence requirement the only requirement?

No. There are other criteria, such as character and, for most applicants, the citizenship test. This page covers only residence.

Worked example

You have lived in Australia on a valid visa for the last 4 years, have held a permanent visa for 18 months, and have been outside Australia for a total of 7 months in those 4 years, of which 80 days fall in the last 12 months.

  • 4 years on a valid visa: yes.
  • Permanent visa for the last 12 months: yes (18 months).
  • Absence: 7 months is no more than 12 months in 4 years, and 80 days is no more than 90 in the last 12 months: yes.

Had the last 12 months included 100 days abroad, the 90-day limit would not be met.
