Learn: how days are counted

Short explainers on the day-count rules behind visas, tax residence and citizenship, each with a worked example and links to the official sources. Day counts only, not tax or legal advice: check with an adviser.

Explainers

  • Schengen 90/180 rule: how the days are counted
    A rolling 180-day window with a 90-day limit; entry and exit days both count.
  • UK Statutory Residence Test: how days are counted
    A day counts when midnight finds you in the UK, with exceptions.
  • UK indefinite leave to remain: the 180 days in 12 months rule
    Absences of more than 180 days in any 12 months can break continuous residence.
  • US foreign earned income exclusion: the 330 full days test
    330 full days abroad in any 12 consecutive months.
  • Canadian citizenship: the 1,095 days physical presence rule
    1,095 days in Canada in the 5 years before you apply.
  • What is a 183-day rule?
    There is no single rule: 183 days is a common tax threshold.
  • US substantial presence test: the weighted 183-day formula
    31 days this year and a weighted 183-day total.
  • Canadian permanent residents: the 730 days in 5 years rule
    730 days in Canada in a 5-year period to keep PR status.
  • UK naturalisation: the 450 and 90 day absence limits
    Absences capped at 450 days over 5 years and 90 days over the last 12 months.
  • US naturalization: continuous residence and physical presence
    5 years of continuous residence, 913 days of physical presence.
  • Irish naturalisation: the 70 day absence allowance
    Up to 70 days' absence in the qualifying year, plus a discretionary 30.
  • Australian citizenship: the residence requirement
    4 years lawful, 12 months permanent, and absence limits.
