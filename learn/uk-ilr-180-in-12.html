UK indefinite leave to remain: the 180 days in 12 months rule

For settlement routes that use Appendix Continuous Residence, your continuous residence is broken if, within any 12-month period, you are outside the UK for over 180 days, unless a permitted reason applies. If your permission was granted from 11 January 2018 onwards, the 12 months are rolling: any 12-month period counts, not just fixed years. Only whole days count as absence.

Quick answers

Is it a fixed year or any 12 months?

If your permission was granted from 11 January 2018 onwards, any 12-month period that includes your absences. For permission granted before that date, Home Office guidance uses consecutive 12-month periods ending on the application date.

Do part days count?

No. The Home Office guidance says only whole days count and part-day absences of less than 24 hours are not counted.

Do separate trips add up?

Yes. Absences are combined, so several trips inside one 12-month period together must not exceed 180 days.

Are there exceptions?

Yes. The guidance lists permitted absences, for example compelling or compassionate circumstances. Whether one applies to you is a decision for the Home Office, so take advice.

Is the limit different for Long Residence?

For Appendix Long Residence, absences that started before 11 April 2024 are judged against 184 days for a single absence and 548 days in total. From that date the 180-days-in-12-months limit applies.

Worked example

The Home Office guidance gives this case: someone is absent for 180 days in a 12-month period, starts the journey back on day 180 and arrives on day 181. Day 181 is not a day of absence, so the period does not exceed 180 days.

A second case: you are away for 100 days from February to May and for 90 days from August to November of the same year. Both fall inside one 12-month period, so the total is 190 days, which is over 180. Unless a permitted reason applies, this would break continuous residence. Taking the same trips more than 12 months apart would not.
